2. The Arithmetic Mean ($\bar{x}$)

The **mean** is the balance point of a dataset. It sums every observed value and distributes the total equally across all observations.

Sample Mean Formula: $$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x_i}{n} = \frac{x_1 + x_2 + \dots + x_n}{n}$$ Where $\sum x_i$ is the sum of all values, and $n$ is the sample size.

3. The Median ($\tilde{x}$)

The **median** is the exact $50^{\text{th}}$ percentile of an ordered dataset. Exactly half of the observations lie above it, and half lie below it.

How to find the Median:
  1. Sort all values from lowest to highest.
  2. If $n$ is odd, the median is the exact middle element.
  3. If $n$ is even, the median is the mean of the two central elements.

5. The Mode (Most Frequent)

The **mode** is the value that occurs with the highest frequency in a data set. Unlike mean and median, the mode can be used for nominal (categorical) data.

Special Properties of the Mode:
  • A dataset can have one mode (unimodal), two modes (bimodal), or multiple modes.
  • If every value occurs with equal frequency, the dataset has no mode.

6. Choosing the Right Measure

Selecting the optimal measure of central tendency depends on the shape of your data distribution and the presence of extreme outliers:

Distribution Shape Recommended Metric
Symmetric / Normal Mean ($\bar{x}$)
Skewed / Has Outliers Median ($\tilde{x}$)
