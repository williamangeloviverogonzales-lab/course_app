1. What is Mathematical Economics?

Lecturer: William Angelo V. Gonzales | August 13, 2024

🎯 Intended Learning Outcomes:

  • Differentiate mathematical economics from non-mathematical economics.
  • Differentiate mathematical economics from econometrics.

Mathematical economics is an approach to economic analysis in which the economist makes use of mathematical symbols in the statement of the problem and draws upon known mathematical theorems to aid in reasoning.

Mathematical economics employs mathematical techniques beyond simple geometry, such as matrix algebra, differential and integral calculus, differential equations, and difference equations. The goal of this course is to introduce you to the fundamental aspects of these mathematical methods encountered across economic literature.

2. Mathematical vs. Non-Mathematical Economics

Mathematical economics is not a separate branch of economics in the sense that public finance or international trade is; rather, it is an approach to economic analysis.

Advantages of Mathematical Economics over Non-Mathematical Economics:
  • Concise and Precise Language: The mathematical language used is concise and precise, reducing the risk of unintentional ambiguity or vague verbal reasoning.
  • Rich Arsenal of Theorems: There exists a vast body of established mathematical theorems ready to aid in deductive reasoning.
  • Predetermined Axioms: It forces the analyst to state all assumptions explicitly through a predetermined set of axioms, preventing unnecessary or hidden assumptions.
  • Higher-Dimensional Analysis: It allows us to analyze multi-variable systems with higher dimensions, which are virtually impossible to visualize using simple 2D or 3D geometry.

In order to take advantage of these mathematical tools, you must first acquire those tools and learn the mathematical language of economics.

3. Mathematical Economics vs. Econometrics

While both fields apply mathematics to economics, they differ in their focus, methodology, and treatment of data:

Econometrics is based upon the development of statistical methods for estimating economic relationships, testing economic theories, and evaluating and implementing government and business policy.

Common applications include forecasting macroeconomic variables such as interest rates, inflation rates, and Gross Domestic Product (GDP). Econometrics evolved as a separate discipline because it focuses on problems inherent in collecting and analyzing nonexperimental economic data.

Key Distinctions:
  • Econometrics: Concerned mainly with the measurement of empirical economic data using statistical methods of estimation and hypothesis testing (inductive study).
  • Mathematical Economics: Refers to the application of mathematics to the purely theoretical aspects of economic analysis, with little or no concern for statistical problems like measurement errors (deductive study).

🐍 Python Application: Theoretical Modeling vs. Empirical Estimation

This snippet contrasts a theoretical mathematical model with an econometric empirical estimation:

import numpy as np

# 1. MATHEMATICAL ECONOMICS (Deductive / Theoretical Model)
# We state a pure theoretical relation: Demand Q = 100 - 2P
def theoretical_demand(p):
    return 100 - 2 * p

print("Theoretical Q at P=15:", theoretical_demand(15))


# 2. ECONOMETRICS (Inductive / Empirical Estimation)
# Observed market data (Prices & Quantities with empirical noise)
prices = np.array([10, 15, 20, 25, 30])
observed_q = np.array([82, 68, 61, 49, 38])

# Estimate linear relationship via regression (Slope & Intercept)
slope, intercept = np.polyfit(prices, observed_q, 1)

print(f"Empirical Estimated Model: Q = {intercept:.2f} + ({slope:.2f})*P")
