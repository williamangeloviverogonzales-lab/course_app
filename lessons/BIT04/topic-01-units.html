1. The $125 Million Unit Mistake

On September 23, 1999, NASA lost contact with the Mars Climate Orbiter as it attempted to enter orbit around Mars. The $125 million spacecraft burned up in the Martian atmosphere.

"The root cause of the loss of the spacecraft was the mismatch between English units (pound-force) used by thruster programmers and SI units (Newtons) expected by navigation software." — NASA Investigation Team

In industrial technology, engineering, and manufacturing, unit accuracy is not just academic—it prevents catastrophic failures, costly machine breakdowns, and material waste.

2. What Physics Is & Physical Quantities

Physics is the branch of science that describes how the physical world operates based on objective evidence determined through observation and experiment. At its core, physics provides the basic principles governing modern industrial technology—from smartphones and electric motors to hydraulic lifts.

Definition 2.1: Physical Quantity
A physical quantity is a property of a phenomenon, body, or substance that can be quantified by measurement.

In simple terms: If you can measure it, it’s a physical quantity! (e.g., length of a beam, mass of raw material, duration of a cycle).

3. Units of Measurement (SI vs. Imperial)

To express a physical quantity meaningfully, a number alone is not enough. We must attach a standardized unit.

Definition 2.2: Unit of Measurement
A standard quantity used to express a physical quantity.
  • International System of Units (SI): Used globally in science, engineering, and technology. Built upon 7 base units (e.g., meter, kilogram, second).
  • Imperial System (British / US Customary): Primarily used in the US and specific industrial equipment. Uses feet, pounds, gallons, and inches.

5. Derived Quantities

Derived Quantities are formed by combining fundamental quantities through mathematical relationships like multiplication or division.

  • Area: $ ext{Length} imes ext{Length} \quad ( ext{m}^2)$
  • Volume: $ ext{Length} imes ext{Length} imes ext{Length} \quad ( ext{m}^3)$
  • Velocity (Speed): $ rac{ ext{Length}}{ ext{Time}} \quad ( ext{m/s})$
  • Density: $ rac{ ext{Mass}}{ ext{Volume}} \quad ( ext{kg/m}^3)$
  • Force: $ ext{Mass} imes ext{Acceleration} \quad ( ext{N} = ext{kg}\cdot ext{m/s}^2)$

8. Unit Conversion Factors

When converting between different systems (SI to Imperial), we multiply by a conversion factor equal to 1.

Since $1 ext{ inch} = 2.54 ext{ cm}$, the conversion factor ratio is: $$\left( rac{2.54 ext{ cm}}{1 ext{ in}} ight) = 1 \quad ext{or} \quad \left( rac{1 ext{ in}}{2.54 ext{ cm}} ight) = 1$$ Always arrange the fraction so that the original unit cancels out in the denominator!

# Unit Conversion Helper Function

def meters_to_feet(m):
    return m * 3.28084

def psi_to_kpa(psi):
    return psi * 6.89476

def ms_to_kmh(ms):
    return ms * 3.6

print(f"3.5 meters = {meters_to_feet(3.5):.2f} feet")
